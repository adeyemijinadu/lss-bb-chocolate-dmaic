# Project Charter

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Project title |Improving Cupping Quality Consistency in Green Coffee Bean Intake. |
| Problem statement |Of the coffee bean lots received from suppliers between 2010 and 2018, approximately 14% (13.8%) score below 80 points on the Total Cup Points scale — the Specialty Coffee Association's threshold for specialty-grade classification. Lots scoring below this threshold are treated as commodity-grade and sold at a lower price than specialty-grade lots would command, representing an estimated loss of approximately $739,000 over this period (based on a sourced price gap of $1.06/kg between average specialty ($7.50/kg) and commodity ($6.44/kg) green coffee prices). Beyond the direct financial impact, receiving and processing sub-specialty-grade lots as if they were specialty-grade exposes the company to reputational and customer-trust risk. |
| Goal statement |Upon implementation, reduce the rate of coffee bean lots scoring below 80 points on the Total Cup Points scale in two phases. Phase 1: within the top origin countries representing approximately 80% of total lot weight (Colombia, Guatemala, Brazil, Honduras, Ethiopia, and Nicaragua), reduce the below-80 rate from the current baseline of 8.4% (by weight) to 5% or below, within 6 months of rollout — an estimated $242,874 in recovered value. Phase 2: extend the same 5% target to the remaining origin countries in the portfolio, with scope, baseline, and timeline to be defined based on Phase 1 outcomes. |
| Business case | |
| Scope (in/out) |**Longitudinal**: From lot receipt/intake (where origin, variety, altitude, and processing method are recorded as fixed lot attributes) through the cupping assessment and specialty-grade classification decision. Upstream farm-level cultivation practices are out of scope, as the dataset captures only static lot-level attributes, not farm process data. **Lateral**: All active origin countries in the supplier portfolio are considered in scope for the overall business problem. Execution is phased: Phase 1 focuses on the origin countries representing approximately 80% of total lot weight (Colombia, Guatemala, Brazil, Honduras, Ethiopia, and Nicaragua — 81.1% of total weight); Phase 2 extends to the remaining origins following Phase 1 implementation. |
